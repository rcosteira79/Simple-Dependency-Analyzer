What kind of "3D" are you imagining for the graph?

This shapes the entire rendering approach we'd use.

A

True 3D — rotatable space

Nodes float in actual 3D space. You orbit the camera with mouse drag, zoom in/out, and nodes have genuine depth (Z axis). Like a constellation you can spin around. Think Three.js or WebGL.

Most immersive. Higher complexity to implement. Can get disorienting with many nodes.

B

2.5D — flat graph with depth effects

A 2D force-directed graph (like D3) but with smooth animations, parallax-style depth on hover, node glow/shadow, and fluid physics when you drag or isolate modules. Looks modern but stays readable.

Much easier to read. Faster to build. Still visually impressive.

C

Either — I care more about the isolation/navigation UX than the 3D effect

The "pzazz" is nice to have but the killer feature is being able to isolate a module and drill down. Pick whatever's more practical to build well.

Pragmatic. Lets you focus budget on the UX that actually matters.
